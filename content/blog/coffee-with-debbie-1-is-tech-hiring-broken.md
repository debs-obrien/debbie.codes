Hi everyone, and welcome to Coffee with Debbie. I don't even drink coffee, but I wanted to create a little podcasty kind of thing, just to get some thoughts out there, connect with some people and maybe help some people along the way. I've been mentoring a couple of women lately and I keep seeing the same things coming up, so this is maybe the best way I can help more people.

In this first episode I talk about hiring, and how hiring is broken. It's an interesting topic for me because I'm going through the process myself. And no, this is not a scary video. I'm being very particular about where I want to work and what kind of role I want, so please don't take what I'm going through as "this is the way the industry is".

## DevRel and living out of a suitcase

For DevRel now, it's super high on travel. It's like they want you to pretty much live out of a suitcase, traveling to events nonstop. Before I joined Microsoft I was doing a hell of a lot of conferences, and I was told I couldn't do that many, because there's so much more to DevRel than just speaking. They were right. If you spend all your time giving talks, you don't have enough time to be close to the product, help shape it, or create the videos and workshop material.

Some people do live out of suitcases and do amazing jobs, and you all rock. But not everybody can do that. A lot of DevRel roles are based in SF, and now Europe is asking for people to travel 40% of the time too. That's about five months of the year on the road. I've turned away from some jobs because I can't do that anymore. I love traveling and I still bring the boys as many places as possible, but I can't be away from my family that much.

## The San Francisco bias

I get that most companies want you in San Francisco. It's a bit like the village pub. Back in the day in the local villages in Ireland, that's where you got the jobs. If my dad went to the pub, he came back with a job fixing someone's kitchen. If he didn't go, he didn't get the job. I think that's the kind of networking happening in San Francisco.

But there's a whole world of amazing developers who never live in or go to San Francisco, and they matter too. Maybe a developer advocate only needs to go once a year for a week of events. We're building more startups and more cool products, so we need more DevRel, and if we can't hire people who don't live on the road or in SF, we're going to run out of them.

## The end of remote work?

Remote is getting even harder now. Everybody wants hybrid or back in the office. COVID changed how a lot of companies thought because we had no choice, and now the talent pool is bigger, so it's "let's all be centred". I do get it. Things are moving fast, and having some people in the office and some not is a challenge. I've lived through that. But it can be worked around, especially for certain people. Every company has to figure out what works for them.

## The toll of long interview loops

I've been interviewing for three months now. I honestly thought I'd have a job in a couple of weeks. When the nos keep coming, you start asking yourself: is this the way the industry is? Am I not good enough? Do I want to continue? It's mentally tough, especially when you have a timeline in your head for when you'd need to change direction.

## Backup plans and staying passionate

I'm lucky and privileged to have another career. I'm a qualified teacher and I could go back to teaching tomorrow, and I've always worked two jobs. But my passion is in tech. I came back into this industry after years out of it because this is where I want to be, and I don't want to be pushed out the door because AI decided we're not needed anymore.

## AI is shrinking teams

I do believe we can get more done with fewer people. But we still need people, and there's still a lot to fix. Maybe we should build more and fix more, instead of cutting people so we can keep doing the same thing with fewer of them. I've spoken to people who have been out of work for over a year, and hiring managers then question why you haven't been working. Losing out at a final interview by one little thing is still heartbreaking when you've poured your heart and soul into it.

## How I apply for jobs with AI

I use Grok Bot for a lot of my stuff. My job hunt bot sends me what's out there every morning and afternoon. I don't apply to all of these jobs. I apply to very few. I spend a couple of days going through the company, the role and who works there, and sometimes I have a chat with people who work there before I've even applied.

Then I think "right, this product looks really cool, let's build some demos". My DevRel bot and I "work for that company for the day": where are the gaps, who's the community, what are they missing, what's good and what's not. I create a Notion doc for each company. Yes, my bot builds the demos for me, because there's a lot to do in a short time. But as soon as it's built one, I play with it, look at the code and say "teach me what you just did". Sometimes I jump on a call with it and we walk through it together. I come out knowing how to use that product.

I could just read the docs and follow the commands. But I believe that next year everybody is going to have their own personal AI assistant, and they're going to build with and learn your product this way too. So I want to see if I can be successful this way, and if not, how the product could improve. It's exciting when a product has a great agentic way in.

It takes time though, and sometimes after all that I still get a no. That's really annoying, but you move on to the next one. I've got Notion docs full of stuff, and I've started making videos to share what I've learned. I highly recommend using AI to help you build demos and learn, although some companies might prefer you to hand code.

## Recruiters and empathy

I've had some really nice hiring managers and recruiters, and some really nice emails saying no. I've also had things go quiet, or take a really long time with no check-ins. We're not yet at the point where my agent is interviewing with your agent. Right now we're still humans. The best recruiters are there for you as a person, guide you along the way and let you down gently if it doesn't work out.

We need to think about the human behind the job application, the interview and the coding challenge: the person putting in all that effort just to get a chance. Maybe someone is a little too junior or missing one thing. With AI you can get someone up to speed in days, so instead of leaving a position open for six months, maybe we should give them a chance.

## The future of personal AI assistants

A lot of people are submitting applications through AI now. Grok Bot actually spotted that some applications have a field meant to catch agents filling them in, and it knows not to fill it in, because otherwise the application gets filtered out. I do understand that hiring managers and recruiters are overwhelmed by all these CVs. It's not easy on either side.

## Bring back the human touch

So we need to make it more human, better, easier and nicer. I don't remember interviewing ever being this horrible, and everyone I talk to is fed up with interviews. I loved my interviews at Microsoft. Even if you didn't get the job, they made it feel nice. We need to be nice.

## Advice if you're job hunting

If you're out there looking for work, I understand how you're feeling. Use your network as much as possible. Talk to people, be online, send messages, and don't give up. Keep creating content, especially if you're in DevRel, and show what you can do. Keep learning and look at different areas, because DevRel, our industry and our lives are all going to change big time.

We don't know what's around the corner, so let's help each other out and be the community we are. If you see someone looking for a job, share that one link with them, even if you've sent it to 15 people. I've had an amazing community helping me these last couple of months, and I'm so grateful. People and the human touch are the most important thing we have right now. I love AI and I love what I do, but the human touch is so much more important.

If there's something you want me to talk about next, let me know. Thanks for watching, and good luck, everyone.
